• MERLIN: A LoRa-Based Infrastructure for Bidirectional Crisis Communication Between Civil and Public Authorities

    Item Type Journal Article
    Author Laura Stojko
    Author Mario Silaci
    Author Michael Grabatin
    Author Johann Altgenug
    Author Wolfgang Hommel
    Abstract During large-scale blackouts, mobile communication infrastructures often fail, limiting coordination and information exchange. This problem is particularly critical in rural areas with a dispersed population, where communication between citizens, authorities, and emergency services becomes difficult. This paper presents MERLIN, a LoRa-based communication infrastructure designed to enable bidirectional crisis communication during blackouts. Drawing on crisis informatics, the system’s potential to support collaboration among stakeholders is examined using a classification matrix for social software, focusing on coordination among citizens, public authorities, and emergency services during blackouts. Specifically, the system is designed to allow authorities to receive emergency alerts, coordinate response activities, support emergency planning, and disseminate critical information to citizens. The paper presents an example of bidirectional crisis communication operating over a purpose-built LoRa-based wireless mesh network. It discusses its implications for crisis informatics research and illustrates how decentralized communication infrastructures can support coordination and information exchange during blackouts.

      “Crisis Informatics” as a keyword

      Introduction

      • Critical infrastructure becomes damaged during extreme weather. Prolonged blackouts strain battery-enabled systems

      • MERLIN (messaging with regional LoRa infrastructure) implements low-power wide-area radio (LPWAN)

        • Implemented in Austria on the border with Slovenia
        • “developed in order to overcome communication issues between local authorities, emergency services, and citizens.” (Stojko et al., 2026, p. 2)
        • “Our solution focuses on contributing to the phase of managing the crisis during the event, especially responses to a crisis event, which can be described as follows according to Pearson and Clair (1998): The coordination of stakeholders and information dissemination that can impact individual and collective reactions, including shattered assumptions, impaired cognitive, emotional, and behavioral responses, and eroded social structure.” (Stojko et al., 2026, p. 2)
      • Crisis informatics ““views emergency response as an expanded social system where information is disseminated within and between official and public channels and entities.” (Palen et al., 2009)” (Stojko et al., 2026, p. 2)
      • ‘LoRa less costly and easier to deploy’ than satellite based systems
      • The author claims often LoRa has been used in IoT or similar situations, not to establish communications, or for disaster relying on centralized systems

      System

      • System includes crisis management software, multiple bases, messengers, RDCP (Roloran Disaster Communication Protocol) protocol to connect components for data exchange

        • 10 stationary and mobile bases, interconnected by radio for redundancy

          • Bases serve as public emergency stations, displaying received messages and allows interaction by keyboard
          • Users follow a process to submit emergency reports
          • Bases battery powered and solar-charged, locations chosen to maximize coverage, accessibility, and sunlight

        • Mobile messengers are private devices with external power allowing citizens to send/receive within range

          • Touchscreen and keyboard allows receipt of information, send reports
          • Official communications signed by public-key
          • messages between device pairs are encrypted, stored locally

        • Crisis management software is the communication interface for authorities and task forces, deployable on ‘common operating systems’ at any location in the network’s range

          • Allows filtering, annotation, categorization, or invalidation of emergency reports
          • Collects telemetry data of locations, link quality, and battery status of bases
          • Allows the provisioning of new messengers

      • RDCP: text message based, bidirectional communication system, open source, incorporates security features such as AES256

        • Allows for private and official announcements, per-device status updates
        • Uses multiple radio channels (unlike MeshTastic/MeshCore) and uses star-based routing over a mesh
        • Ack based retransmission

      Stakeholders

      • “According to the classification matrix for social software in crisis management by Reuter et al. (2012), MERLIN is designed to enable bidirectional crisis communication among authorities and citizens (crisis communication), among authorities (inter-organizational crisis management), and between citizens and authorities (integration of citizen-generated content).” (Stojko et al., 2026, p. 5)

        • Communication among citizens is not the intent of the system, but supported
      1. Authorities: organize supportive measures, share information with citizens, manage crisis response by overview of the situation, emergency response planning
      2. Citizens: receive information about crisis, respond to assessment questions, report emergency needs, provide ability to help others in resolving crisis
      3. Emergency services: receive priority information/emergency needs, available resources, and request support from other services

      Ex: citizens can issue an emergency report via messenger or base station with urgent need for water

      Ex: Authorities broadcast evacuation instructions to messenger devices and public bases

      Discussion

      • Challenges in successful implementation include an intuitive GUI for interacting in a high stress environment with unfamiliar technology

        • Low bandwidth and high latency implemented over RDCP (using undedicated frequencies)
        • Simple messages may be delayed several minutes due to hardware/software limits
        • Monitoring for misuse (such as a follow up questions after use) require significant effort (above a phone-call)
